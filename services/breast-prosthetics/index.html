Breast Prostheses and Silicone Restoration

Restoring symmetry and confidence with compassionate, dignified care

What is it?

Breast prostheses and custom silicone prosthetics restore symmetry and body image following mastectomy, lumpectomy, trauma, or congenital absence. They may include facial or partial limb restorations, designed with realistic aesthetics and functional outcomes in mind.

How can it help?

  • Restoring natural body contour and symmetry
  • Improving clothing fit and comfort
  • Reducing postural imbalance
  • Supporting emotional wellbeing
  • Enhancing confidence and self-image

Common questions

Breast Prostheses and Silicone Restoration: your questions answered

How soon after my mastectomy can I wear a breast form?

A soft fabric form can usually be worn within days of surgery, and a silicone prosthesis at around six to eight weeks, once the wound has healed and any radiotherapy is finished. Cancer Research UK puts the permanent fitting at about six weeks after surgery. The soft form, sometimes called a comfie or a softie, is light and does not press on the scar. If your chest is still swollen after radiotherapy, wait until the swelling settles, because a form fitted to a swollen chest will not fit the chest you end up with. Your surgeon or breast care nurse has the final say on timing.

How much does a breast prosthesis cost, and does medical aid pay for it?

Prices vary widely by type, so we quote you before anything is ordered or made. A soft post-surgical form is inexpensive, a standard silicone form costs more, and a custom silicone prosthesis made to your own contour costs more again. Many medical schemes contribute towards an external breast prosthesis and mastectomy bras from a prosthesis or external medical items benefit, usually with a prescription or motivation, though cover differs between schemes and plans. If you are not on a scheme, Reach for Recovery's Ditto Project supplies silicone prostheses to state patients, and CANSA distributes free hand-knitted forms through its care centres.

Can I just buy a breast form online myself?

Yes, and for many women that is a perfectly sensible choice. If you had a straightforward single mastectomy, you know your bra size, your chest is fully healed and you are matching a fairly standard shape, an off-the-shelf silicone form from a reputable supplier will do the job and you do not need us for it. Come to us when the standard forms do not work: a very uneven chest, a partial or reconstructed breast, skin changed by radiotherapy, a form that keeps riding up, or when you want a nipple prosthesis or a custom silicone form. Buying twice costs more than being measured once.

Do I need a special mastectomy bra?

Not always, but the bra matters more than most women expect. A prosthesis only sits correctly if something holds it against the chest wall, so a well fitting bra with a firm band and a full cup does most of the work. Mastectomy bras have sewn-in pockets that stop the form shifting, and Cancer Research UK notes you can also have a pocket stitched into a bra you already own. If your current bras fit properly and hold the form still, keep them. If the form slides, rides up or you are forever adjusting it, the bra is usually the problem, not the form.

I had a lumpectomy, not a mastectomy. Is there anything for me?

Yes. A partial form, also called a shaper or a shell, fills the dip left after a lumpectomy or after radiotherapy has shrunk the breast, so both sides sit level under clothing. These are small silicone or foam pieces shaped to sit over the top, the outer side or the underside of the breast, and they go into an ordinary bra or a pocketed one. Partial forms are the most commonly missed option after breast conserving surgery, largely because nobody mentions them. They are also used after reconstruction, when one side has ended up smaller than the other.

Will a silicone form be too heavy? My neck and shoulder already ache.

Lightweight silicone forms exist for exactly this reason, and they are worth asking about if you have lymphoedema, osteoporosis, sensitive skin or ongoing neck and shoulder pain. One widely sold lightweight range, Amoena's Natura Xtra Light, is described by its manufacturer as nearly 40% lighter than comparable silicone forms. Be careful of the claim you will read everywhere that a weight-matched form protects your posture. A study measuring lower back muscle activity in women after mastectomy found no link between prosthesis weight and that activity. What weight does affect is how the form sits, whether it stays level with the other side, and whether you will wear it all day.

How long does a breast form last and how do I look after it?

Cared for properly, a silicone form lasts several years, usually longer than its guarantee. Most carry a two-year manufacturer warranty against defects. Wash it daily in warm soapy water and pat it dry, store it in the moulded cradle inside its box, and keep it away from rings, pins, pet claws and anything sharp, because a puncture lets the silicone leak. Body lotion, perfume and sunscreen break down the outer film, so keep them off it. You can swim in a standard form if you rinse it in clean water afterwards, since chlorine and salt degrade silicone. Dedicated swim forms are made for this.

Can I be fitted at home instead of coming in?

Yes. Fittings are done at the consulting rooms in Morningside, Sandton, at your home, or at your hospital bedside across Johannesburg, Sandton, Midrand, Centurion and Pretoria. A first fitting means undressing to the waist in front of a stranger, at a point when you may not yet want to look at your own chest, so many women prefer their own bedroom, their own mirror and their own bras to try against. Appointments are private and unhurried, and you are welcome to bring someone with you. Bring the bras you actually wear every day, not the good one.
